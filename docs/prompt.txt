A vintage 1950s-style retro-futuristic radio commercial and news bulletin, heard through a damaged AM radio speaker during an active nuclear war. Constant layered chaos in the background: heavy tape hiss and crackling static, a low electrical hum, distant explosions and rumbling shockwaves, air-raid sirens wailing and rising and fading, fast Geiger counter clicking that speeds up and slows down, radio signal dropouts and interference screeches between each story, the occasional burst of falling debris. The audio warbles and distorts as if the broadcast could cut out at any moment. The background noise is loud and relentless but the announcer's voice stays clearly audible on top of it. 

The announcer is an extremely deep, booming, gravelly baritone, like the narrator of a classic movie-trailer or retro commercial. Slow, dramatic and authoritative, with a smooth salesman warmth that is slightly too calm for the chaos around him. Short pauses after each story. On the final line he drops into a low, quiet whisper. 

Announcer: "Attention, M.I.T. students! This is The Wasteland Wavelength, bringing you the latest news over here at M.I.T.! On November nineteenth, physicists revealed that a black hole smaller than an atom could slip inside a star, devour it from within, and set off an explosion we might actually see. On November twenty-third, engineers unveiled an A.I. technique to design analog chips faster and cheaper. That same day, a study of twenty-one languages suggested the human brain shares one common neural code for meaning. On November twenty-fifth, researchers warned that draining peatlands leaves a long legacy of warming. Also on November twenty-fifth, a study tied the power behind America's data centers to about six hundred and fifty early deaths in a single year. And today, November twenty-seventh, scientists announced engineered antibiotics that could widen the pool of new drug candidates. Study hard, students. Those sirens are only a drill... probably. This has been The Wasteland Wavelength, new episodes every [DAY]. Stay curious. Stay safe. Stay... underground." 

End with a massive explosion, a screech of static, a fading signal, then silence.
